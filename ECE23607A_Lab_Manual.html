Introduction to MATLAB Simulink

Simulink is a software package for modelling, simulating, and analysing dynamical systems. It supports linear and nonlinear systems modelled in continuous time, sampled time, or a hybrid of the two. Systems can also be multirate — with different parts sampled or updated at different rates.

For modelling, Simulink provides a graphical user interface (GUI) for building models as block diagrams using click-and-drag mouse operations. This allows drawing models just as textbooks depict them. Simulink includes a comprehensive block library of sinks, sources, linear and nonlinear components, and connectors. Custom blocks can also be created.

Models are hierarchical — providing insight into how models are organized and how parts interact. After defining a model, it can be simulated using a choice of integration methods from Simulink menus or via MATLAB command window. Simulation results can be stored in the MATLAB workspace for post-processing and visualization.

Starting Simulink

  1. 01Open MATLAB and type simulink in the command window, or click the Simulink icon in the toolbar.
  2. 02Create a new model via File → New → Model or by clicking Blank Model in the Simulink Start Page.
  3. 03Open the Simulink Library Browser to drag and drop blocks into the model canvas.
  4. 04Connect blocks by drawing lines between output and input ports, configure block parameters, and run the simulation.

Generation of Basic Signals

Aim: To generate Sine, Square, Triangular and Ramp waveforms using Simulink and observe the outputs.
Theory

Sine Wave: A continuous, periodic signal characterized by a smooth, oscillatory waveform. Fundamental in analog communication systems, serving as carriers for AM, FM, and PM.

Square Wave: Features sharp transitions between high and low discrete levels. Composed of odd harmonics; used as clock signals for digital synchronization and PWM.

Triangular Wave: Has linearly increasing and decreasing slopes. Used in FM synthesis and PLL circuits for frequency tracking and synchronization.

Ramp Signal: Exhibits linearly increasing/decreasing amplitude over time. Used for pulse shaping and in radar systems for LFM waveform generation.

DPSK Transmitter and Receiver in Simulink

Aim: To generate and detect DPSK signals using Simulink.
Theory

DPSK (Differential Phase Shift Keying) encodes information in the phase difference between consecutive signal elements, rather than using absolute phase values. No reference signal is required at the receiver.

Modulator: A differential encoder (XOR gate) encodes input data; the encoded signal is multiplied with a carrier (and its 180° inverted version) and combined to produce the DPSK signal.

Demodulator: The received DPSK signal is multiplied with its one-bit delayed version, passed through an LPF and decision block to recover the original binary data.

DPSK Encoding Example

ParameterSequence
Binary Data–0010010011
Diff. Encoded10110110111
Phase of DPSK0π00π00π000
Detected Binary–0010010011
Simulink Model 
  Z⁻¹ ──────────────────────────► XOR ──────────────────────────────►
  Delay                            ▲     XOR Operation

  Bernoulli Binary ──────────────► NOT ──► (×) Gain──────► Add ──► Scope
  Generator            │                   Product                  ▲
                        └───────────────────────────── (×) Product──┘

  Carrier ──► (×) Product2 ──► FIR Filter ──► Relational ──► Z⁰ Delay1
  Add1 ──────────────────────────────────────► Operator   ──► XOR Op.
Fig 2d — Simulink DPSK model with Bernoulli Generator, XOR, NOT, Gain, Carrier, FIR Filter, Relational Operator
Procedure
  1. 01DPSK uses differential encoding via an XOR gate. Use Bernoulli Binary Generator for random binary input.
  2. 02Encoded input multiplied with carrier yields ASK for bit "1". Inverted encoded data multiplied with a 180°-shifted carrier yields ASK for bit "0".
  3. 03Add both product outputs to obtain the DPSK output.
  4. 04At decoder: DPSK + carrier → ASK; result filtered with FIR filter, compared to threshold, differentially decoded to recover original data.
  5. 05Observe all five signals in the Scope: Input, Encoded, Carrier, DPSK, and Recovered.
Expected Output

Five-row scope display (time 0–5 s): Input binary stream → Differentially encoded signal → High-frequency carrier → DPSK modulated signal with phase inversions → Recovered binary data matching input.

DPSK Transmitter and Receiver successfully implemented. The recovered signal matches the original binary input.

Multiband Signal using Communications Toolbox

Aim: To generate a multiband signal using the Communications Toolbox.
Theory

Multi-band signals are bandlimited signals whose spectral support is restricted to several continuous intervals called bands. No information resides outside these bands. The technique enables higher data capacity by combining two or more carriers — known as carrier aggregation in 5G/LTE, or channel bonding in Wi-Fi.

Equalization, Convolution & Cyclic Prefix on OFDM

Aim: To perform equalization, convolution and cyclic prefix addition on basic OFDM using MATLAB.
Theory

Equalization reverses distortion incurred by a signal transmitted through a channel, rendering the frequency response flat end-to-end. Used in telephone lines, DSL, and television cables.

Convolution is the primary technique in DSP — it mathematically combines two signals using the impulse response to describe system behaviour.

Cyclic Prefix (CP) is a copy of the end of an OFDM symbol prepended to its beginning. CP length ≥ maximum channel delay spread. It creates a circular convolution that simplifies equalization to a single one-tap frequency-domain equalizer per subcarrier.

Advantages & Disadvantages of CP

Advantages

  • One-tap equalizer per subcarrier
  • Preserves subcarrier orthogonality; eliminates ISI
  • Simple and efficient channel estimation
  • Flexible subcarrier allocation

Disadvantages

  • Reduces spectral efficiency and data rate (overhead)
  • Increases PAPR
  • Requires precise synchronization
MATLAB Code 
clc; clear; close all;

u1 = 1:8;
h  = [0.4 1 0.4];   % Channel impulse response

figure
subplot(2,1,1); stem(u1);  title("Input signal")
subplot(2,1,2); stem(h,'^'); title("Channel impulse response")

N   = length(u1);
yl1 = conv(u1, h);          % Linear convolution
yc1 = cconv(u1, h, N);      % Circular convolution

L   = length(h);
ucp = u1(N-L+1:N);           % Last samples as Cyclic Prefix
u2  = [ucp u1];               % Prepend CP to input
yl2 = conv(u2, h);
yl2 = yl2(L+1:end);          % Remove CP

if max(yc1 - yl2(1:N)) < 1e-8
    disp("Linear and circular convolution sequences match.")
end
With Cyclic Prefix added, linear and circular convolution sequences match — confirming correct OFDM CP implementation.

Low Pass and High Pass Filter

Aim: To design and implement Low Pass and High Pass Butterworth filters in Simulink.
Theory

Low Pass Filter (LPF): Passes frequencies below the cutoff frequency; attenuates higher frequencies. Used for signal smoothing in audio, image processing, and biomedical systems.

High Pass Filter (HPF): Passes frequencies above the cutoff frequency; attenuates lower frequencies. Used for signal sharpening and edge detection.

Procedure
  1. 01Combine Sine Wave generator and noise (Signal Generator) using an Add block to produce a noisy sinusoidal signal.
  2. 02Design Butterworth LPF: order = 8, cutoff = 30 rad/s.
  3. 03Design Butterworth HPF: order = 8, cutoff = 10 rad/s.
  4. 04Connect noisy signal to both filters, then observe all four waveforms (2 inputs + 2 filtered outputs) in the Scope.
LPF successfully removed high-frequency noise, preserving the sine wave. HPF removed the low-frequency sine component, passing only high-frequency noise.

Band Pass and Band Reject Filter

Aim: To design and implement Band Pass and Band Reject filters in Simulink.
Theory

Band Pass Filter (BPF): Combination of HPF and LPF; allows only frequencies in a selected range (f_cL to f_cH) to pass. Used in audio processing, wireless communication, EEG/ECG.

Band Stop Filter (BSF / Notch Filter): Blocks only a selected frequency range; passes all others. Used in noise cancellation and power line interference suppression (50/60 Hz removal).

     │      ████                   │████           ████

       f_cL  f_cH                    f_cL  f_cH
Fig 8a-b — Frequency responses of Band Pass and Band Reject filters
Procedure
  1. 01Combine Sine Wave + noise with an Add block.
  2. 02BPF design: HPF passband = 30 Hz; LPF passband = 40 Hz; passband attenuation = 1 dB; stopband attenuation = 80 dB; sampling rate = 256 sps.
  3. 03BSF design: LPF stopband = 25–35 Hz; HPF passband = 50 Hz; passband ripple = 1 dB; stopband ripple = 60 dB.
  4. 04Connect noisy input to both BPF and BSF in parallel; observe 5-panel scope output.
BPF preserved the sinusoidal component within the passband. BSF removed the sine component while passing noise frequencies outside the stop band.

Scatter Plots & Eye Diagrams of QPSK with AWGN

Aim: To obtain scatter plots and eye diagrams of a QPSK signal in the presence of AWGN.
Theory

Scatter Plot (Constellation Diagram): Shows received signal points in the I-Q plane; reveals modulation quality and noise effects. Ideal QPSK has 4 points at ±0.707, ±0.707. Noise causes points to "spread" around their ideal locations.

Eye Diagram: Overlays multiple segments of a received waveform to assess signal integrity. A wide-open eye indicates low noise; a closed eye indicates severe degradation.

AWGN: Additive White Gaussian Noise — constant power spectral density across all frequencies with Gaussian amplitude distribution. Introduces random phase and amplitude distortion in QPSK.

Procedure
  1. 01Build chain: Random Integer → QPSK Modulator Baseband → (Eye Diagram before noise) → AWGN → (Eye Diagram after noise) → QPSK Demodulator → Scope + Constellation Diagram.
  2. 02Random Integer: set size = 4, sample time = 0.001, samples per frame = 1.
  3. 03QPSK: phase offset = π/4, constellation ordering = Grey, input type = integer.
  4. 04AWGN: input seed = 67, SNR = 10 dB.
  5. 05Compare eye diagrams with and without noise; observe constellation scatter.
Expected Output 
  Eye Diagram WITHOUT noise:    Eye Diagram WITH AWGN (SNR=10dB):

    Clean, wide eye opening        Partially closed eye
    ─────/\────/\────             Multiple noisy trajectories
         │    │                   Eye opening reduced

  Constellation WITHOUT noise:  Constellation WITH AWGN:
    4 precise points at           4 point-clouds with spread
    45°, 135°, 225°, 315°         proportional to noise level
Fig 9b-e — Eye diagram and constellation: clean vs noisy QPSK signal
Eye diagrams and scatter plots successfully obtained. AWGN causes visible degradation — eye partially closes and constellation points spread into clouds.

OFDM Modulation and Demodulation

Aim: To perform OFDM Modulation and Demodulation in Simulink and calculate BER.
Theory

OFDM (Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing) divides a high-rate data stream into multiple parallel lower-rate subcarriers, each modulated at an orthogonal frequency. This combats multipath fading and ISI — used in Wi-Fi (802.11a/g/n/ac/ax), LTE, 5G, DVB-T, and ADSL.

  OFDM Chain:

  Bit stream → PSK/QAM Mapper → S/P Convert → IFFT → Add CP → P/S → Tx

  Rx → S/P → Remove CP → FFT → Equalize → P/S → PSK/QAM Demapper → Bits

  IFFT: maps frequency-domain symbols to time-domain OFDM symbol
  FFT:  recovers frequency-domain data at receiver
Fig 10a — OFDM transmit and receive chain
Procedure
  1. 01Build chain: Bernoulli Binary → QPSK Modulator → IFFT → noise addition → FFT → QPSK Demodulator.
  2. 02Bernoulli Binary: initial seed = 61, sample time = stime, samples/frame = 200, output = double.
  3. 03QPSK: input type = bit, M array number = M, phase offset = π/M.
  4. 04FFT and IFFT: implementation mode = Auto.

Viva Questions

EXP 01 — Generation of Basic Signals
Q01What is Simulink, and how is it different from MATLAB?▶
Simulink is a graphical programming environment within MATLAB for modeling, simulating, and analyzing dynamic systems. Unlike MATLAB which is text-based, Simulink uses block diagrams for system representation, allowing drag-and-drop model construction.
Q02What types of basic signals can be generated in Simulink?▶
The basic signals include: Sine Wave, Square Wave, Sawtooth Wave, Step Signal, Pulse Signal, and Ramp Signal — all available from the Sources library.
Q03What is a sine wave and where is it used?▶
Sine waves are continuous, periodic signals with smooth oscillatory waveforms. Used as carriers in AM, FM and PM communication systems; also in AC power supply, signal processing (Fourier analysis), and medical imaging (ultrasound, MRI).
Q04What parameters can be configured in the Sine Wave block?▶
Amplitude, Frequency (Hz), Phase (radians), and Sample time.
Q05What are the applications of the ramp signal?▶
Ramp signals are used in control systems for stability analysis, oscilloscope sweep control, motion control (uniform acceleration), radar LFM waveform generation, and image processing (edge detection).
Q06What is a square wave and what are its applications?▶
Square waves have sharp transitions between high/low levels, composed of odd harmonics. Used as digital clock signals, in PWM for motor speed/LED control, and for testing digital circuits.
EXP 02 — DPSK
Q01What is DPSK?▶
DPSK (Differential Phase Shift Keying) encodes information in the phase difference between consecutive signal elements, eliminating the need for a reference carrier at the receiver.
Q02How does DPSK differ from PSK?▶
PSK uses absolute phase values to encode data; DPSK uses relative phase differences between successive symbols, allowing non-coherent detection without a reference carrier.
Q03What is the main disadvantage of DPSK?▶
Higher BER compared to coherent PSK. Also, errors in one bit can propagate to the next bit due to the differential encoding scheme.
Q04What happens if the delay block is removed in DPSK demodulation?▶
The receiver cannot compare phase differences between consecutive symbols, leading to incorrect demodulation and high bit error rates.
Q05What are practical applications of DPSK?▶
Wireless communication (Wi-Fi, Bluetooth), satellite and optical communication, RFID systems, and powerline communication.
EXP 04 — Noise & Histograms
Q01What is Gaussian noise and what are its characteristics?▶
Gaussian noise has amplitudes following a normal (bell-curve) distribution with mean=0 and a specified variance. Its histogram is symmetric and bell-shaped, representing the most common noise type in electronic systems.
Q02What is Uniform noise and how does its histogram differ from Gaussian?▶
Uniform noise has equal probability for all amplitude values within a specified range. Its histogram is flat-top (rectangular), unlike the bell-shaped Gaussian histogram.
Q03What does AWGN stand for and what does it mean?▶
Additive White Gaussian Noise — "additive" because it adds to the signal, "white" because it has constant power spectral density across all frequencies, and "Gaussian" because amplitude values follow a normal distribution.
EXP 05 — OFDM Basics
Q01What is the purpose of the Cyclic Prefix in OFDM?▶
The Cyclic Prefix acts as a guard interval to absorb ISI from multipath propagation. It converts linear convolution with the channel into circular convolution, enabling simple one-tap equalization per subcarrier in the frequency domain.
Q02What happens if the Cyclic Prefix is too short?▶
If CP length is less than the channel delay spread, it cannot fully absorb the ISI, causing signal distortion and degraded system performance (increased BER).
Q03What is the difference between linear and circular convolution?▶
Linear convolution of sequences of length N and M produces an output of length N+M-1. Circular convolution produces an output of length N (same as input). With CP added to make it match length N, linear convolution result equals the circular convolution result.
EXP 06 — QPSK
Q01What is QPSK and how does it differ from BPSK?▶
QPSK (Quadrature PSK) transmits 2 bits per symbol using 4 phase states (45°, 135°, 225°, 315°). BPSK transmits 1 bit per symbol using 2 phases. QPSK doubles spectral efficiency without increasing bandwidth requirement.
Q02What is Grey coding in QPSK?▶
Grey coding maps dibits to constellation points such that adjacent points differ by only one bit. This minimizes bit errors when a received symbol is mistaken for a neighboring symbol — only 1 bit error occurs instead of 2.
Q03What is the role of AWGN in the QPSK Simulink model?▶
The AWGN Channel block simulates realistic channel conditions by adding Gaussian noise to the modulated signal. The SNR parameter controls noise level; lower SNR causes higher BER and more spread in the constellation diagram.
EXP 07 & 08 — Filters
Q01What is the significance of cutoff frequency in a filter?▶
The cutoff frequency defines the boundary between passband and stopband. At the cutoff frequency, the output power is reduced by half (–3 dB) relative to the passband level.
Q02What are the advantages of Butterworth filters?▶
Butterworth filters have a maximally flat response in the passband (no ripple), smooth roll-off in the transition band, and are relatively simple to design. They are the most common choice for general-purpose filtering.
Q03How does filter order affect performance?▶
Higher order → steeper roll-off, sharper transition between passband and stopband, more complex implementation. Lower order → gradual roll-off, simpler design but poorer frequency selectivity.
Q04How is a band-pass filter designed from simpler filters?▶
Combine an HPF and LPF in series such that the HPF cutoff frequency is lower than the LPF cutoff frequency. The overlap defines the passband (f_cL to f_cH).
Q05What are the applications of band-pass and band-reject filters?▶
Band-pass: audio processing, wireless communication, biomedical (EEG, ECG). Band-reject: noise cancellation, power line interference suppression (removing 50/60 Hz hum), radio communication.
EXP 09 — Eye Diagrams & Scatter Plots
Q01What is an eye diagram and how is it interpreted?▶
An eye diagram overlays many segments of a received waveform. The "eye opening" indicates signal quality — a wide eye means good SNR and clear timing margins; a closed or narrow eye indicates high noise, ISI, or timing jitter.
Q02How does SNR impact scatter plot and eye diagram?▶
Higher SNR → well-defined constellation points, widely open eye. Lower SNR → constellation points spread into clouds, eye opening closes. At very low SNR, the eye may close completely indicating unreliable detection.
Q03What is Symbol Error Rate (SER) in QPSK?▶
SER is the probability of incorrect symbol detection; it increases as the noise level increases (lower SNR). For QPSK, each symbol carries 2 bits, so SER is related but not equal to BER.
EXP 10 — OFDM Modulation & Demodulation
Q01What is OFDM and why is it used?▶
OFDM (Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing) divides a data stream into multiple parallel subcarriers, each modulated at a lower rate. It improves spectral efficiency, reduces ISI, and provides robustness against multipath fading. Used in Wi-Fi, LTE, 5G, DVB-T, and ADSL.
Q02What is orthogonality in OFDM?▶
Orthogonality ensures that subcarriers do not interfere with each other at sampling instants, even though their spectra overlap. This allows efficient bandwidth utilization without Inter-Carrier Interference (ICI).
Q03What is the role of IFFT and FFT in OFDM?▶
IFFT (at transmitter) converts frequency-domain subcarrier symbols into a time-domain OFDM symbol for transmission. FFT (at receiver) transforms the received time-domain signal back to the frequency domain for decoding each subcarrier.
Q04Key applications of OFDM?▶
Wi-Fi (802.11a/g/n/ac/ax), LTE and 5G cellular, DVB-T digital television, ADSL/VDSL broadband, and underwater acoustic communications.
